Asking the right question

The question "Venlo or plastic tunnel?" is really hiding a more fundamental one: What will you grow, for how long, and in what climate?

These two greenhouse types are not the cheap and expensive versions of the same thing — they are different tools optimised for different production conditions. The right choice is not determined by budget but by production goals, regional climate, cropping system and investment horizon. Investors who decide based on the price difference alone often find themselves a few years down the line either constrained by a structure that can't meet their needs, or realising they could have made a better choice from the start.

Plastic tunnel: low entry barrier, high flexibility

A plastic tunnel greenhouse is a structure formed by polythene (PE) film stretched over galvanised steel or pipe arches. Ventilation is provided through side openings or roof vents; motorised ventilation is available on some models.

Key advantages:

  • Initial investment cost is significantly lower than a Venlo structure.
  • Construction time is short; the structure is mobile and can be dismantled and moved to a different plot.
  • Obtaining operating permits for agricultural subsidy applications is generally faster.
  • Financial entry risk is lower for seasonal or short-term production.
  • Practical for gaining experience on a smaller scale before committing to a larger investment.

Main limitations:

  • PE film must be replaced every 3–5 years, which is a recurring maintenance cost.
  • Thermal insulation is lower than glass or twin-wall polycarbonate; heating costs can be high in cold climates.
  • Gutter height is typically limited (2.5–3.5 m), which may not provide sufficient headroom for tall high-wire crops.
  • Automation and irrigation infrastructure can be integrated retrospectively, but if not planned from the outset, retrofit costs and constraints increase.
  • In large areas, the structural system is less efficient than Venlo; labour flow and equipment movement can become difficult to manage.

Venlo greenhouse: the reference structure for long-term commercial production

Venlo is a modular steel structure of Dutch origin with vertical walls clad in glass or polycarbonate (PC). Multiple spans are arranged side by side, bringing large areas under a single roof. It is the most widely used commercial greenhouse architecture in the world.

Key advantages:

  • Light transmission is highest with glass cladding, providing a yield advantage for light-sensitive crops.
  • High gutter height (4–6 m and above) accommodates high-wire cropping systems, crop baskets and mechanised movement.
  • When built correctly, structural lifespan can reach 20–30 years, lowering annual cost per unit area over time.
  • Automation, climate control, fertigation and supplementary lighting systems are designed for seamless integration.
  • When built with twin-wall PC, energy consumption can be optimised; heating costs in cold climates can be lower than with single glass or PE.

Main limitations:

  • Initial investment cost is significantly higher than a plastic tunnel.
  • Planning, soil investigation, structural engineering and permitting processes require more time and resources.
  • Repairs after damage are more complex and costly, especially in glass-clad structures.
  • On very small plots (below roughly 500–1,000 m²), the modular expansion advantage is difficult to exploit and economies of scale are lost.
Practical guide: A Venlo investment typically makes sense when all of the following conditions apply: a production plan of 10 years or more, commercial scale, crops that require high-wire culture or intensive climate control, and sufficient equity or financing capacity. If not all of these conditions are met, a plastic tunnel is usually the more sensible starting point.

Four key criteria in the decision process

1. Crop type and cultivation system

In high-wire culture (climbing species such as tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers and beans), plant height can reach 3–4 metres. The limited gutter height of a plastic tunnel frequently conflicts with this requirement. For low-growing crops such as lettuce, leafy vegetables, seedlings or flowers, tunnel height is generally adequate. What you plan to grow directly determines what infrastructure you need to build.

2. Regional climate

In cold regions with long winters — such as the Black Sea coast or inland and eastern Anatolia — heating costs are high regardless of greenhouse type. In these conditions, the better thermal retention of a Venlo structure — particularly with twin-wall PC — can translate into meaningfully lower fuel consumption over the long term. In Mediterranean or Aegean climates, the weaker heat retention of a plastic tunnel rarely causes significant problems in winter, though summer temperature management can become challenging.

3. Production horizon and scale plan

If there is a realistic chance of switching to a different crop, a different location or a smaller operation within 3–5 years, the portability and lower initial cost of a plastic tunnel become genuine advantages. If the plan is permanent, commercial-scale growth, the long lifespan and flexibility of a Venlo structure eventually balance out the unit annual cost; an early Venlo investment eliminates capacity bottlenecks in later years.

4. Automation and infrastructure plan

If you intend to add fertigation, CO₂ enrichment, supplementary lighting or an environmental control system in future years, designing this infrastructure from the outset is more practical and economical. A Venlo greenhouse is designed to facilitate this integration. Retrofitting a plastic tunnel is possible, but it introduces additional costs, load-bearing capacity constraints and installation challenges. Planning the irrigation system and fertigation infrastructure in harmony with the greenhouse type minimises surprises after construction.

How to make the right decision

Across both greenhouse types, the single most critical factor determining success is the fit between crop, climate and scale. There is no universally "better" greenhouse; there is only the greenhouse that best suits your production model and investment timeline.
